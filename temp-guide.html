01

EMI in one sentence

An equated monthly instalment is a scheduled payment that repays part of a loan principal and pays interest on the outstanding balance. If the rate and tenure stay fixed, the EMI normally stays the same while its internal principal-and-interest split changes.

The lender is not charging the full-loan interest every month. Interest is calculated on the balance still unpaid, which is why a reducing-balance schedule matters.

02

Why early EMIs contain more interest

At the start of a loan, the outstanding principal is at its highest. The first monthâs interest is therefore larger. After each payment reduces the principal, the next monthâs interest is calculated on a slightly smaller balance.

Over time, more of the same EMI goes toward principal. An amortisation schedule makes that change visible year by year.

03

What changes the EMI or total cost?

Three inputs drive the standard reducing-balance calculation.

  • Principal: borrowing more raises the EMI and total interest.
  • Interest rate: a higher rate increases the cost of every remaining instalment.
  • Tenure: a longer tenure can reduce the monthly EMI but may increase total interest because the balance remains outstanding for longer.
04

Where prepayments fit

A prepayment reduces the outstanding principal earlier than scheduled. Future interest is then calculated on a smaller balance. Depending on the lender, a prepayment may reduce the tenure, reduce the EMI, or allow a choice between the two.

Always check the loan agreement for prepayment charges and the lenderâs method. A general EMI calculator does not model every lender-specific rule.

Questions, answered

Frequently asked questions

01 Is EMI the same as total loan cost?

No. EMI is the monthly payment. Total loan cost also depends on the number of payments, total interest, processing fees and any other lender charges.

02 Why can a lender quote differ from a calculator?

Payment dates, daily interest conventions, rounding, fees, insurance and floating-rate resets can create differences.

03 Does a lower EMI always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A lower EMI caused by a longer tenure can lead to more total interest even though the monthly payment is easier to manage.
